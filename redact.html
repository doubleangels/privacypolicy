1. Overview

Redact is an open-source Android app built by DoubleAngels that removes EXIF and container metadata (GPS location, device details, timestamps, camera settings, and similar hidden data) from photos and videos, lets you inspect that metadata before sharing, and converts media formats, all without leaving your device.

This Privacy Policy explains, in plain language, exactly what Redact does and does not do with your data. Because Redact is designed around local-only processing, the short version is simple: the app doesn't want your data and doesn't collect it. The rest of this page explains why, in detail, including every permission the app requests, every third-party library it ships with, and the rights available to you under applicable privacy law.

2. Who We Are

Redact is developed and published by DoubleAngels, an independent developer. DoubleAngels does not operate a company that buys or sells personal data, does not run an advertising business, and does not maintain any server that stores your photos, videos, or identity. For the single, opt-in category of diagnostic data described in Section 6, DoubleAngels acts as the data controller under applicable data protection law.

You can reach DoubleAngels directly using the contact details in Section 16, read every line of Redact's source code on GitHub, and review this policy's own revision history, since it is published from that same public repository.

3. Information We Collect

We collect none of your personal information. Redact has no account system, no sign-in, no registration, and no server that stores or receives your photos, videos, metadata, or identity. You can verify this directly, since the INTERNET permission is the only one that lets the app reach the network at all, and it is used for exactly one optional, opt-in purpose described in Section 6.

Specifically, Redact never does any of the following.

  • Upload your photos, videos, or any media file to any server, for any feature.
  • Read, log, or transmit the EXIF/GPS metadata it scans or removes.
  • Require or collect your name, email address, phone number, or any account identifier.
  • Use advertising identifiers, behavioral analytics, or any ad SDK.
  • Track you across apps or websites, or build any kind of profile about you.

The only data that ever leaves your device is the optional, anonymized crash-diagnostic data described in Section 6, and only if you explicitly turn that setting on.

4. Permissions Explained

Android requires apps to declare permissions up front, but declaring a permission does not mean Redact uses it to collect data about you. Here is exactly what each permission is for and whether it's required:

Permission What it's used for Status
READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE Lets the system gallery picker show your photos/videos on Android 12 and earlier, where the modern document picker isn't available. Android 12 and below only
READ_MEDIA_IMAGES / READ_MEDIA_VIDEO Optional alternative to Android's system document picker, which Redact uses by default on Android 13+ and which needs no media permission at all. Optional
READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED Lets you (on Android 14+) grant Redact access to only specific photos/videos you choose, instead of your entire library. Optional
ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION Lets the Scan screen display the exact GPS coordinates embedded in a file, so you can see what's there. Not required to remove that same GPS data in Clean. Optional
POST_NOTIFICATIONS Shows progress and completion notifications for Clean/Convert jobs. Optional, off by default
FOREGROUND_SERVICE / FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PROCESSING Keeps a long-running Clean or Convert batch job alive and shows its progress notification when notifications are enabled. Used only if notifications are on
INTERNET The only permission that allows network access at all. Used exclusively to send scrubbed crash/error diagnostics to Sentry, and only when you turn on Send Crash Reports in Settings. Used only for opt-in crash reporting

Redact never requests broad, standing access to your entire media library by default. On Android 13+, the app's primary file-selection method is Android's built-in document/photo picker, which hands Redact only the specific files you choose. The app never sees the rest of your gallery.

Every permission Redact has ever been granted can be reviewed and revoked at any time from Android's own Settings app, under Apps > Redact > Permissions. Revoking an optional permission simply disables the feature it supports; Redact continues to work for everything else.

5. Push Notifications

Redact can show local notifications for job progress and completion, controlled by the Notifications setting, which is off by default. These are ordinary Android notifications generated on your device by the app itself through a local foreground service. Nothing about them is pushed from a remote server.

Redact does not use Firebase Cloud Messaging or any other push notification service, has no server capable of sending you a push message, and never registers a device token, push token, or advertising identifier anywhere for this or any other purpose. You can also mute or customize Redact's notification channels at any time from Android's own Settings app, independently of the in-app toggle.

6. Optional Crash Reporting

Redact includes an optional crash and error reporting feature powered by Sentry. It is controlled by a single setting, Send Crash Reports, and is off by default. Nothing is sent to Sentry, and Sentry's SDK is not even initialized, unless you explicitly switch this setting on.

When you turn it on, Redact applies the following safeguards before anything leaves your device:

  • No PII by default: the SDK's default-PII collection is explicitly disabled (sendDefaultPii = false), and broad device-context and automatic-breadcrumb collection are turned off.
  • No screenshots or UI capture: screenshot attachment and view-hierarchy attachment are disabled, so a crash can never capture an image of what was on screen.
  • Automatic scrubbing: before any event, log, metric, or breadcrumb is sent, Redact's own scrubbing layer strips content:// and file:// URIs, filesystem paths, GPS coordinate pairs and latitude/longitude fields, and file names from every message, tag, and stack frame. These patterns are redacted even in error messages generated deep inside system or library code.
  • Allow-listed tags only: custom diagnostic tags are restricted to a fixed, reviewed list (for example operation_type, mime_type, processing_success, video_width). Arbitrary free-text values are never attached as tags.
  • Your media is never touched: Sentry has no access to the photos or videos you process. Only device model, OS version, app version, a scrubbed stack trace, and the allow-listed tags above may be included.

You can turn crash reporting off again at any time in Settings, and doing so immediately stops any further data from being sent. Diagnostic data sent to Sentry is governed by Sentry's own Privacy Policy.

8. Advertising, Tracking, and Automated Decision-Making

Redact shows no advertising and contains no advertising SDK of any kind, so there is nothing here to opt out of. The app does not use the Android Advertising ID, does not build behavioral or interest profiles, and does not share or sell data to ad networks, data brokers, or analytics platforms, because it does not collect the data such profiles would require in the first place.

Redact does not use cookies, since it is a native Android app rather than a web page, and it performs no cross-app or cross-site tracking. The app also makes no use of automated decision-making or profiling that produces legal or similarly significant effects. Every action in Redact, cleaning, scanning, or converting a file, is a local operation that you trigger directly, and nothing about your usage influences any decision made about you.

9. Where Your Files Go

Cleaned and converted files are saved to Pictures/Redact or Movies/Redact in your device's own MediaStore, ordinary folders you control, back up, or delete like any other photo or video on your phone. Redact never relocates these files off your device.

When you share a file into Redact from another app, a temporary stripped copy is created in the app's private cache, handed to your chosen share target, and then deleted shortly afterward. Settings let you optionally overwrite temporary files (1, 3, or 7 passes) before deletion for extra assurance, though this has limited effect on modern flash storage, as noted in the app itself. Clear Cache on Startup automatically removes cache files older than 24 hours, and you can clear temporary files manually from Settings at any time.

When you uninstall Redact

Android sandboxes every app's private data, settings, and cache. Uninstalling Redact immediately removes all of it, including your Settings choices, such as whether crash reporting was enabled, and anything left in its temporary cache. Because allowBackup is disabled, none of this is copied to Android's automatic cloud backup first. Files Redact already saved to Pictures/Redact or Movies/Redact are your own MediaStore files and are not deleted by uninstalling the app, the same as photos saved by your camera.

If you had enabled crash reporting before uninstalling, previously sent diagnostic events may still exist in Sentry's systems for the duration of their own retention period. You can contact Sentry directly, or email us using the details in Section 16, to ask about deleting them.

10. Security

Because Redact processes your media locally and has no account system or backend database, there is no central server holding your photos, videos, or personal information for an attacker to compromise. The app's network access is limited to the single opt-in diagnostic channel described in Section 6, which uses HTTPS.

The app disables cleartext network traffic entirely (usesCleartextTraffic="false") and disables Android's auto-backup of app data (allowBackup="false") so app state can't be extracted through device backups. There is no password, PIN, or credential of any kind stored by Redact, since there is no account to protect. Because the full source code is public, anyone can independently audit these claims rather than take our word for it.

13. Do Not Track Signals

Some web browsers send a "Do Not Track" signal to websites. Redact is a native Android app rather than a website, so it has no way to receive that browser signal, and it performs no tracking for the signal to disable in any case. If you have enabled optional crash reporting, turning it back off in Settings has the same practical effect as opting out, since it immediately stops any further diagnostic data from being sent.

14. Your Rights

Legal basis for processing (GDPR)

Where the GDPR applies, the only processing Redact performs, sending optional diagnostic data to Sentry, is based on your consent, given by actively switching on Send Crash Reports in Settings. You can withdraw that consent at any time by switching it back off, which stops any further processing immediately and does not affect the lawfulness of anything already processed beforehand.

European Economic Area, UK, and Switzerland (GDPR)

If you are located in the EEA, UK, or Switzerland, data protection law gives you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict, or port your personal data, and to object to its processing. Because Redact does not collect or process personal data about you in the first place, most of these rights have nothing to act on. If you believe any data about you has reached us, for example through a message you sent, contact us and we will address it promptly.

International data transfers

If you enable crash reporting, the limited diagnostic data described in Section 6 may be processed by Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.) on servers located outside your own country, including in the United States. This only happens if you opt in, and only covers the scrubbed technical data described in Section 6, never your photos, videos, or identity. Sentry's own privacy policy describes the safeguards it applies to such transfers.

California (CCPA/CPRA)

Redact does not sell or share personal information, and does not collect personal information as defined by the CCPA/CPRA in the ordinary course of operating the app. Accordingly, there is no sale or sharing to opt out of.
